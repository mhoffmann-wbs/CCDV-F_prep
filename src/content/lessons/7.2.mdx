Expect *"which combination of controls"* and *"what should the design include"* questions. The answer is almost always **several independent layers**, with the strongest guarantees enforced **outside** the model.

## Key concepts

### 1. Content policy

Write down what your app may and may not do: topics, actions, tone, and how to refuse. Then express that policy in three places:
- **The system prompt**, which states the boundaries and gives a refusal script.
- **Classifiers or screens** that check input and output against the same policy.
- **Operational responses**: monitor refusals, and throttle or ban repeat offenders.

### 2. Layered guardrails

| Layer | Examples | Fails how |
|---|---|---|
| **Input** | Harmlessness or injection screen with a small, fast model, size limits, PII redaction | Novel attacks get through |
| **Model** | System-prompt policy, untrusted-content isolation, structured outputs | Instructions are advisory |
| **Tools** | Least-privilege tool set, strict schemas, authorization checks in code, human approval for risky actions | Over-broad tools do damage |
| **Output** | Validation against a schema, leak and PII filters, moderation before display | Filters miss paraphrases |
| **Infrastructure** | Sandbox or container, egress allowlist, credentials injected by a proxy, rate and spend limits | Misconfiguration |
| **Monitoring** | Logs, traces, alerts, regular red-teaming | Nobody looks at it |

Because each layer fails differently, an attack has to beat **all** of them at once.

### 3. Secure by design

- **Privacy**: collect and send only the data you need, redact before logging, set retention, and keep tenants isolated.
- **IAM**: authenticate users in your app. The agent acts with the user's rights or narrower, and each workload gets its own identity (see 7.4).
- **Least privilege**: few tools, read-only where possible, only the directories and endpoints it needs, and no secrets the model can see.
- **Safe defaults**: deny by default, ask a human for anything irreversible, and fail closed when a guardrail errors.

## Exam traps

- **One perfect prompt as the whole guardrail strategy.** Prompts guide behavior, but they don't enforce it.
- **Adding filters without removing excess capability.** Removing a tool the use case doesn't need beats screening its misuse.
- **Fail-open guardrails**: "if the classifier times out, let the request through."
- **Logging raw transcripts full of PII "for debugging"** in the name of observability.
- **Granting broad permissions** (such as bypass mode) to "avoid prompts" in production.

## On the field

**Fan chat, layered.** Fan messages pass an injection and abuse screen first. The model gets a policy prompt ("stats, schedules and rules only; refuse betting advice") and two **read-only** tools. Replies are checked for leaked prompt text and personal data before display. The container can reach only the stats API, through a proxy that injects credentials. A dashboard tracks refusal spikes per user. When an injection gets past the first screen, it still finds no write tool, no secret and no open egress.
